

### Draft Motion for Summary Judgment

Before drafting, the app should ask the user two things, in this order, before any drafting begins:

1. **"Do you believe your complaint is strong enough, with nothing else to add to your case?"** — a simple yes/no gate. If no, don't proceed to drafting yet — this isn't the right moment for this motion.
2. If yes: **"Do you want me to take everything from your complaint and draft it into a Motion for Summary Judgment, treating your submitted evidence as accurate?"** — explicit confirmation before the AI call that actually drafts anything.

**Jurisdiction-aware procedural context (informational, not directive):** Before or alongside this, the agent should check the case's stored jurisdiction and give general procedural information about typical timing — summary judgment motions are commonly filed after discovery, but in some jurisdictions they can be filed earlier, and rules vary. Present this as general procedural information about how this motion typically works in the relevant jurisdiction — not as a personalized legal conclusion telling the user they are or aren't allowed to file. This mirrors how the Glossary handles jurisdiction-specific facts: informational, not advisory.

**Substantive requirement to reflect in the drafting logic:** summary judgment is appropriate when there's no genuine dispute over the material facts — the user's evidence should already be submitted/complete before this motion makes sense. If the user indicates there are still disputed facts, the agent should ask about those specific disputes as part of the upfront question set (see execution note below) — not loop back into a conversation after drafting has started.

### Draft Motion for Discovery

The agent looks at the case's jurisdiction and procedural posture, then drafts a motion requesting to proceed to discovery — the standard procedural step for exchanging evidence before trial. Same principle applies: confirm intent and gather any necessary specifics upfront, then draft.

### Execution Principle for Both Motions (and generally, going forward)

**All necessary questions must be asked upfront, before any drafting call is made** — not through back-and-forth after a draft has started. The goal is one clean round: gather every piece of information the agent needs (confirmations, disputed-fact details, jurisdiction context already on file), then make a single drafting call. This keeps AI usage as low as possible per document and avoids the frustrating "starts drafting, has to stop and ask something, draft again" pattern.

## 11. New Feature: Defense Button

Add a **Defense** button to the Assembly screen, using a **two crossed swords icon**.

**Purpose:** lets the user respond when the opposing side has filed something against their case.

**Flow when tapped:**

1. Prompt: **"What did the defense file?"**
2. User submits the filing — either by uploading a document directly, or by taking photos of the physical document they received.
3. **Extraction:** the agent reads the submitted filing and pulls out any useful information not already stored in the case — particularly the defense's name, email, and address, if present. This information should be saved so it's available for future filings and for strengthening existing motions, without the user having to re-enter it later.
4. **Understanding why:** the agent needs to understand the substance of what the defense filed — why they filed it, what they're arguing — before it can draft a response. If the filing alone doesn't make this clear, this may prompt a short interview with the user. This step costs 1 credit, consistent with the existing credit philosophy (real AI analysis work costs a credit; simply answering questions does not — but here the analysis of the filing itself is the paid step).
5. **Drafting the response:** once the agent understands what was filed and why, it helps the user draft a responsive motion addressing the defense's claims — defending the user's case against whatever was raised.

This follows the same execution principle as the motions in Section 10: gather what's needed (the filing + any clarifying interview) upfront, then draft — not draft-then-interview-then-redraft.

# Appendix A: Fee Waiver / In Forma Pauperis — Fallback Intake Template

*(Referenced in Section 6 above. This is the full content of that fallback template, included here in full rather than as a separate document.)*

## When This Triggers

This intake flow is a **fallback only**. It should run when the AI agent searches for the user's specific jurisdiction's fee waiver / in forma pauperis form and cannot locate it online. This will not always happen — many jurisdictions' forms are findable — but when the agent comes up empty, this generic intake ensures the user isn't stuck with nothing.

## Required Disclaimer Language (must display before and after this intake)

Do not reference any specific state. Use language along these lines:

> "This is a broad, general template for a fee waiver / in forma pauperis request. It is not built for any one jurisdiction, and it may or may not be accepted exactly as-is by your local court. We use this only when we couldn't locate the specific form for your court online — that's possible, but won't always happen.
>
> Once this is filled out, your information will be saved in the app, easy to find. If your court requires their own specific paper form, you can simply copy your saved answers over onto that form when you get to your local courthouse — most of the work is already done. Worst case, you're copying information you already have, not starting from scratch."

## Closing Message (display after the user completes this intake)

> "You're doing a good job."

This should appear as a simple, warm closing line after submission — not buried in disclaimer text, its own clear moment.
