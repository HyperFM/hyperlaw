
HyperLaw — Complete Rebuild Specification (Final)

Vision

HyperLaw is not a chatbot.

It is an AI-powered legal case building platform that helps people organize their facts, understand the law, and generate legal documents in a calm, structured workflow.

The goal is to reduce the mental burden of legal matters by guiding users through one simple task at a time.

Every screen should answer one question:

“What do I do next?”

The user should never feel overwhelmed by seeing multiple unrelated tools competing for attention.

⸻

Core Design Rules

Throughout the application, follow these principles:

* One task per screen.
* One primary button per screen.
* One clear objective before moving to the next stage.
* AI should not interrupt users while they are entering information.
* AI should activate only after structured information has been collected.
* Every screen should feel calm, focused, and easy to understand.
* The workflow should always show users what their next step is.

The experience should feel closer to Duolingo than a legal dashboard.

PHASE 1 — Parties

This replaces the current “Add Incident” starting point.

The first thing every user should do after creating a case is identify the people involved.

Nothing else should appear on this screen.

No AI.

No documents.

No motions.

No deadlines.

No jurisdiction.

No uploads.

Only party collection.

⸻

Collect

For each person:

* First name
* Last name
* Official or Civilian

If Official:

* Agency
* Title
* Badge number (if known)
* Location (if known)

If identity is unknown:

Allow:

* John Doe
* Jane Doe

Display this disclosure:

Use Doe placeholders only when a person’s identity is genuinely unknown. Officials can often be identified later through discovery. This is informational only and not legal advice.

AI must never generate or infer names.

This screen is structured data entry only.

⸻

Voice Reference System

Replace the color and number tagging system.

Every party automatically receives a simple voice-friendly nickname.

Purpose:

* Improve speech recognition.
* Reduce dictation errors.
* Reduce stress.
* Make storytelling easier.

Example:

🥒 Pickle → Officer Jonathan Smith

🐟 Fish → Officer Maria Lopez

🍎 Apple → Jane Brown

🥜 Peanut → John Doe

👾 Monster → Officer Robert Davis

The nickname is displayed beside the legal name throughout the workspace.

PHASE 3 — Tell Your Story

Replace “Add Incident.”

Rename this screen:

Tell Your Story

The page should be intentionally minimal.

Display:

* Large orange animated microphone.
* Large text field.
* Small instructions.

The microphone should activate the device’s built-in keyboard dictation.

Do not build a custom transcription service.

No paid speech APIs.

Use native speech-to-text.

Users simply tell the story.

Example:

Pickle grabbed my wrist while Fish stood nearby. Monster walked over and told Pickle to place me in handcuffs.

No AI processing occurs while speaking.

No interruptions.

No clarifying questions.

The AI waits until the user presses Submit.

PHASE 5 — AI Assembly

Only after:

* Parties
* Court
* Timeline
* Facts

have been completed.

AI responsibilities:

* Build complaint.
* Match facts to legal claims.
* Organize allegations.
* Associate supporting evidence.
* Detect factual gaps.
* Organize the case logically.

The AI should not conduct intake.

The AI should assemble information.

⸻

PHASE 6 — Learning Index

Automatically generated.

Read-only.

No chat.

The AI should extract every legal allegation from the complaint.

For each allegation display:

* Applicable statute.
* Constitutional provision (if applicable).
* Relevant case precedent.
* Plain-English explanation.
* How that authority may relate to this specific fact pattern.

Examples:

* Excessive Force
* False Arrest
* Retaliation
* First Amendment
* Fourth Amendment

This updates automatically as the complaint evolves.

⸻

PHASE 7 — Tutor

Tutor has one responsibility:

Conversation.

Not legal research.

Not document generation.

Not intake.

Not case law.

Tutor should provide:

* Encouragement.
* Organization.
* Practical guidance.
* Plain-English explanations.
* Emotional support while remaining professional.

If the user reveals new facts during conversation:

Prompt:

“You mentioned something that isn’t currently in your case. Would you like to add it?”

Never silently modify the case.

Always ask permission first.

⸻

PHASE 8 — Documents

After the complaint exists:

Generate:

* Complaint
* Timeline
* Motion
* Declaration
* Affidavit
* Demand Letter
* Other pleadings

These should use the structured case database instead of repeatedly asking users the same questions.

⸻

PHASE 9 — Evidence Modules

Move these outside the primary workflow.

Examples:

* Video Evidence
* Audio Evidence
* Medical Records
* Police Reports
* Exhibits
* Photographs

These are optional organizational tools.

Do not force users through them during intake.

⸻

Upload Shortcut

If the uploaded document is already a complaint or legal filing:

Skip:

* Parties
* Court Selection
* Story
* Timeline

Automatically:

* Extract parties.
* Detect the court.
* Build timeline.
* Detect legal claims.
* Populate the case.
* Build Learning Index.

The uploaded complaint becomes the starting point.

⸻

Existing Bug Fixes

Before implementing additional Tutor features:

Fix the Tutor response failure.

Current behavior:

“I couldn’t generate a response.”

Investigate:

* AI API calls.
* Error handling.
* Conversation state.
* Timeouts.
* Prompt routing.
* Retry logic.

The Tutor should fail gracefully and provide a helpful message if the AI service is unavailable.

⸻

Additional Cleanup

Remove or relocate anything that distracts from the primary workflow.

Specifically:

* Generated documents should not appear before a complaint exists.
* Litigation motions should not appear before users have facts entered.
* Deadlines should not appear until users intentionally open the deadline section.
* AI analysis should not appear before intake is complete.
* Tutor should not perform intake.
* Learning Index should never become a chat interface.

⸻

Long-Term Product Philosophy

HyperLaw is designed to help people organize and understand legal matters, not replace attorneys or provide individualized legal representation. Every feature should reduce cognitive load and guide users through a structured process.

Whenever there is a choice between adding another feature or simplifying the workflow, choose simplicity. Users facing legal issues are often already overwhelmed. HyperLaw should feel like a calm guide that helps them move forward one step at a time, turning a complex legal process into a series of manageable tasks.
