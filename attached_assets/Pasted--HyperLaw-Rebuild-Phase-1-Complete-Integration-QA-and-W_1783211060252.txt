
HyperLaw Rebuild — Phase 1: Complete Integration, QA, and Workflow Validation

Objective: Before adding any new major features, make HyperLaw feel like one polished, unified application. Do not begin Phase 2 until every workflow is stable.

1. Full Application Audit

Perform a complete audit of the entire application.

Test every workflow from beginning to end, including:

* User registration and login
* Subscription and billing
* Case creation
* Case editing
* Case deletion
* Parties workflow
* Voice nickname assignment
* Court selection
* Tell Your Story (native device dictation)
* Timeline Builder
* Timeline editing
* AI Timeline generation
* Tutor conversations
* Document uploads
* Existing document generation
* Reminder system
* Notes
* Saving and editing
* Navigation between workflow stages
* Returning to previous stages
* Mobile and desktop layouts

Fix every issue found before continuing.

⸻

2. Integrate the New Workflow

The rebuild introduced new workflow screens.

Integrate them naturally into the existing application.

Do not simply add new pages.

Instead:

* Connect every stage together.
* Preserve authentication.
* Preserve billing.
* Preserve user accounts.
* Preserve AI services.
* Preserve existing cases through safe migration.

The workflow should feel like it has always been part of HyperLaw.

⸻

3. Simplify the Interface

Every screen should have one clear purpose.

No screen should overwhelm users with multiple unrelated tools.

Workflow:

Create Case

↓

Parties

↓

Court Selection

↓

Tell Your Story

↓

Timeline

↓

Case Review

↓

AI Assembly

↓

Learning Index

↓

Generate Documents

↓

Tutor

↓

Evidence

Only show the tools relevant to the current stage.

⸻

4. Verify Data Flow

The structured case file should become the single source of truth.

Everything should read from and write back to the same structured case.

Verify:

* Parties flow into Timeline.
* Timeline flows into AI Assembly.
* AI Assembly updates the complaint.
* Learning Index updates automatically.
* Documents always pull from the latest structured data.
* Tutor understands current case context without maintaining separate copies.

Avoid duplicate storage or disconnected workflows.

⸻

5. Validate AI Responsibilities

Every AI feature should have exactly one responsibility.

Case Builder

* Collect structured information.

Timeline Builder

* Organize events chronologically.

AI Assembly

* Draft legal documents from structured data.

Learning Index

* Research and explain legal authorities.

Tutor

* Conversation, organization, and guidance only.

Prevent overlapping responsibilities.

⸻

6. Auto-Save and Version History

Implement automatic saving after every meaningful change.

Maintain version history for:

* Story
* Timeline
* Complaint
* AI Assembly

Allow users to restore previous versions if needed.

⸻

7. Mobile Optimization

Optimize specifically for mobile devices.

Verify:

* Native keyboard dictation works smoothly.
* Keyboard never hides buttons.
* Large touch targets.
* Smooth animations.
* Fast loading.
* Resume exactly where the user left off.
* Responsive layouts across supported devices.

⸻

8. Educational Learning Cards

On every major workflow screen include a small:

“Why does this matter?”

button.

These should explain:

* Why identifying parties matters.
* Why court selection matters.
* Why chronology matters.
* Why reviewing the timeline matters.

Keep explanations short, educational, and separate from AI chat.

⸻

9. Performance and Stability

Before Phase 2:

* Zero TypeScript errors.
* Zero console errors.
* No broken routes.
* No unnecessary API calls.
* No crashes.
* No memory leaks.
* Fast navigation.
* Reliable autosaving.
* Graceful error handling throughout the application.

Only begin Phase 2 after the application passes all testing.

⸻

HyperLaw Rebuild — Phase 2: Complete the Core Platform

Once Phase 1 is complete, begin implementing the remaining core capabilities.

⸻

1. AI Assembly

Complete the AI Assembly engine.

Input:

* Parties
* Court
* Timeline
* Story
* Evidence

Output:

* Organized facts
* Draft complaint
* Potential legal claims (clearly labeled as AI-generated suggestions, not legal conclusions)
* Supporting facts linked to each claim
* Identification of missing factual information without inventing facts

The AI must never fabricate facts.

⸻

2. Learning Index

Build a read-only Learning Index.

Automatically organize:

* Applicable statutes
* Constitutional provisions (where applicable)
* Relevant precedent
* Plain-English explanations
* How each authority may relate to the user’s facts

Regenerate automatically whenever the case changes.

This is a research tool, not a chat.

⸻

3. Case Review Screen

Before document generation, present a comprehensive review page.

Display:

* Parties
* Court
* Timeline
* Facts
* Attached evidence
* AI-generated potential claims (clearly labeled as suggestions)
* Missing information

Allow users to edit anything before generating documents.

⸻

4. Upload Shortcut

If a completed complaint or legal filing is uploaded:

Automatically:

* Extract parties
* Detect court
* Build timeline
* Populate the structured case file
* Generate Learning Index

Skip manual intake whenever sufficient information already exists.

⸻

5. Intake Checklist

Replace conversational intake with a structured checklist.

Examples:

* Witnesses
* Photos
* Videos
* Audio
* Medical Records
* Police Reports
* Body Camera Footage
* Property Damage
* Financial Loss
* Emotional Harm
* Filing Deadlines

Each item opens its own focused form and can be completed later.

⸻

6. Evidence Modules

Keep evidence management outside the primary workflow.

Examples:

* Videos
* Photos
* Audio
* Medical Records
* Police Reports
* Exhibits

These remain optional organizational tools.

⸻

7. Continue Your Case Dashboard

Expand the Home screen.

Display:

* Progress indicator
* Current workflow stage
* Next recommended action
* Case Health checklist
* Most recently edited case

Users should always know what to do next.

⸻

8. Voice Reference System Refinement

Continue improving the voice reference system.

* Automatically assign simple voice-friendly nicknames such as Pickle, Fish, Peanut, Apple, Monster, Rocket, Tiger, Lemon, Cookie, Pumpkin, etc.
* Allow users to replace the nickname with another simple word.
* Encourage distinct words that improve speech recognition.
* During dictation, users speak only the nickname.
* AI automatically maps nicknames back to the correct legal names before analysis.
* Generated legal documents must always use the person’s real legal name or Doe placeholder—never the nickname.

⸻

9. Final Product Goal

HyperLaw should function as a guided legal workspace—not just a chatbot or document generator.

Its purpose is to:

* Reduce cognitive load.
* Help users organize complex legal matters.
* Teach users procedural concepts in plain English.
* Produce structured, editable work product from the user’s own information.
* Keep every feature connected through a single structured case file.

Success Criteria: A first-time user should be able to create a case, identify the parties, select the court, tell their story using native dictation and voice-friendly nicknames, review an AI-generated timeline, review the assembled case, understand the legal research through the Learning Index, and generate editable documents—all without feeling overwhelmed or losing track of what comes next.
