Exhibit Generation

After sufficient information is collected, Claude automatically builds an exhibit using the existing HyperLaw template.

Each exhibit should contain structured sections similar to your examples, such as:

* Headline
* Supporting quote
* Key observations
* Timeline context
* Relevant parties
* Evidence references
* User-selected legal authorities (where appropriate)
* Footer with exhibit number and references

The system should present factual observations and user-provided information without asserting legal conclusions or facts not supported by the record.

Storage Strategy (Important)

I actually would not store everyone’s hour-long videos on your servers.

Instead:

* Store the project data (markers, transcripts, exhibit drafts, timestamps, settings, metadata).
* Keep the original video on the user’s device by default.
* Let the user relink the original video if needed.
* Offer optional cloud storage later as a premium feature.

This keeps your storage costs much lower while still preserving nearly all of the user’s work. The project file is relatively small; the video is what consumes significant storage.

Let’s also include an optional field called “Why This Moment Matters.”
Instead of reaching legal conclusions, it can summarize the user’s point in plain language, for example:

“This exhibit highlights the sequence of events surrounding this statement and the surrounding context the user believes is significant.”

That helps connect the exhibit to the larger presentation without overstating what the evidence proves.

This entire thing focuses on organizing and presenting information clearly rather than replacing legal judgment.
