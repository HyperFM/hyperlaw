HYPERLAW PLATFORM REDESIGN (Vision Update)

We’re no longer building Legal Screen Builder.

The platform is now officially called:

HyperLaw

This is no longer just a screen generator.

HyperLaw is an AI-assisted legal evidence platform that helps users:

* Analyze evidence
* Understand legal concepts
* Build persuasive visual exhibits
* Organize cases
* Prepare courtroom-ready presentations

The current screen builder is only one module of a much larger platform.

COMPLETE NAVIGATION REDESIGN

The current bottom navigation feels like placeholders.

I don’t want tabs named after current features (“Screens”, “Laws”, “Citations”).

I want the navigation to represent the future vision of HyperLaw.

The bottom navigation should become:

🏠 Home

The user’s dashboard.

* Continue recent case
* Recent projects
* Case statistics
* Recent exports
* Quick actions

⸻

🛠 Build

This is the primary workspace.

Contains:

* Screen Builder
* Evidence Vault
* Projects
* Timeline
* All visual exhibit creation

This is the centerpiece of the application.

⸻

🎓 Tutor

This replaces the current Laws tab.

Tutor is NOT documentation.

Tutor is an AI legal tutor.

It should teach users legal concepts using THEIR OWN FACTS.

Instead of saying:

First Amendment

Tutor explains:

“Based on what you’ve entered…”

Then teaches:

* why the issue may matter
* what facts support it
* what evidence strengthens it
* what questions remain
* what additional evidence could help

Tutor should ask intelligent follow-up questions whenever more context would improve understanding.

The experience should feel like speaking with an experienced legal educator—not reading a textbook.

Tutor should never present legal conclusions as certainty. It should explain legal concepts and connect them to the facts the user has entered.

AI ARCHITECTURE

I no longer want HyperLaw to rely only on static legal libraries.

Integrate AI as a core part of the experience.

The AI should:

* ask follow-up questions
* gather missing facts
* explain legal principles
* recommend possible violations
* recommend exhibit types
* explain reasoning
* teach users throughout the process

The AI should make building exhibits feel conversational instead of filling out forms.

⸻

TUTOR MODE

Tutor should become one of HyperLaw’s defining features.

Example:

User describes an incident.

Tutor responds:

“I noticed several facts that may relate to constitutional or policy issues. Let me explain why.”

Then Tutor teaches using the user’s own evidence.

Example:

You mentioned that you spoke up about an existing policy and were removed shortly afterward. Depending on the circumstances and applicable law, some courts analyze whether protected speech was followed by adverse action. Let’s examine the facts that support—or weaken—that possibility.

Then Tutor asks follow-up questions to help the user understand the legal framework rather than simply labeling the issue.

The goal is education, not legal conclusions.
